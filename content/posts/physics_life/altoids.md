A good physicist or mathematician should always have some chalk around them, preferably *Hagaromo*. It is often the case that one needs to go to another room to give a talk, seminar, or lecture which may not have the best chalk. Indeed, the worst feeling is walking into a lecture hall and realizing the only chalk available is *Crayola*. 

Luckily, one can avoid this problem by carrying chalk with them. This presents the question of, "what is the best way to carry chalk to a different room?"

The answer are Altoids tins. Specifically, the wider Altoid tins with the soft mints. 

![Altoid tin closed](/posts/physics_life/img/tinclosed.jpg)

The Altoid tin pictured above is the perfect size for proper chalk. It closes tightly and the chalk fits snuggly enough to where it won't break. Indeed, I have never had a broken piece of chalk in the tin.

![Altoid tin oepn](/posts/physics_life/img/tinopen.jpg)

The tin is the perfect size to fit into your pocket or just carry in one had. Additionally, buying a tin of Altoids is quite cheap and lasts for a while. I have seen several mathematicians that also carry around Altoid tins of chalk. I personally have been using the same tin for several years now.

That being said, you should be careful not to use other tins. While the tin carrying hard mints looks like it may fit chalk, it turns out to be slightly too small to carry proper chalk.

![Altoid tins closed](/posts/physics_life/img/tinspen.jpg)

The is a pen for scale above. I originally bought the blue tin here to find another container. I flew through the mints by eating several at a time and also giving it out like candy. I was let down when I realized it was too small for chalk.

![Altoid tins open](/posts/physics_life/img/tinsopen.jpg)

Another nice tin is the Altoid cinnamon tin. I hate the cinnamon mints but they make the chalk smell nice afterwards.

I hope this information is useful to someone.
