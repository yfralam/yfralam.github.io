## The physical stɒkes

This morning I woke up to several messages in my group chat saying Navier-Stokes (NS) has been solved, in large part due to AI. Given even nice conditions, the equations still blow up in finite time. 

I don't think that is a surprising fact (if I swirl around the coffee in my mug it never blows up in my face) but it is nice to have a mathematical proof that the equations blow up. One could have anticipated this from numerical simulations as well. 

While of some interest to the field of PDEs, physically there isn't really anything new. The length scales at which this blowup occurs is much smaller than the scales at which the fluid approximation breaks down and we have to start worrying about individual molecules. A nice mnemonic would be that the effective field theory of Navier-Stokes breaks down at some small length scale/high energy. That's not surprising at all.

It's for this reason that the solution doesn't really teach us much physics (imho). U(1) hypercharge has a Landau pole, but it's so far above the Planck scale I know the Standard Model breaks down before getting anywhere close.

At first glance, my reaction seems a bit muted. I think it is no surprise that I don't really care for math for the sake of math, but the combination of just immediately being handed the answer *and* the fact that there doesn't seem to be any physical insight makes this just a tad boring to me personally. Many of the Millennium Prize problems are like this. There are things we know that should be true but don't have rigorous proofs off. Yang-Mills mass gap, for instance. Another would be that P is almost certainly not equal to NP. 

This is not to discount this discovery, it is still amazing that humanity has managed to figure out one of the puzzles, but personally I think I just expected more. 

## Controversy and Drama

Partially the reason for this post is the controversy surrounding how the proof was found. There is no question that AI was used to assist the proof but there is a mess of a situation for who the credit should go to. 

The drama is really that the mathematicians who spent time working on NS (one of which worked with Antrhopic) may have been scooped by OpenAI. The rumors are that OpenAI heard of the mathematicians work and then trained their AI on it. 

As i'm writing this the day of, I don't really know what the situation is like. The statement by the mathematician, Tristan, can be found [here]([https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf](https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf "https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf")). 

If true, this does rub me the wrong way. In academia, scooping someone like this (when you knew of their work) is taboo at best. This would be almost at the level where I'm in the middle of refereeing a paper, take their findings and publish a similar manuscript which may have even improved on theirs. 

Anyways, we'll see what the next few days will bring.

## Tangents off the path to the solution

In part, the usage of AI here is maybe the best case use in math. Humans put together the math for the type of solutions, and AI just brute force searched them all, something a human could never do.

That being said, it is a testament to human ingenuity that we find workarounds that are not just brute force. Oftentimes, failure to a solution teaches us much more than the solution itself. 

Humans cannot brute force solutions, but this is a blessing. It means there needs to be some creativity to get to the solution. New techniques needs to be developed or even new branches of mathematics. This inevitably leads to new discoveries beyond the problem they were designed to help with. For example, the solution to Fermat's last theorem was preceded by an enormous amount of effort put into learning new things and using modular forms and elliptic curves. These are things that we use outside the context of Fermat's last theorem. I have friends that are using the results for Hecke algebras on physics.

So on one hand, it is nice to have a solution. On the other, I hope you cannot blame me for lamenting all of the new mathematics we may have discovered on the road to the NS blowup. There are no tangents of the trivial point.
