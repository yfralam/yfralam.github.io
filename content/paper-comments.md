## \\(\mathrm{Sp}(4,\mathbb Z)\\) actions on 3d \\(\mathrm{U}(1)^2\\) symmetric theories
I was initially brought on by Ryan and Andreas to help figure out a duality web for \\(\mathrm{U}(1)^2\\) theories in 3d. From the 2d side, we expected at least 9 theories related by duality transforms, but quickly found that there were an infite number which did not make sense to classify (one can get an infinite number by just stacking a bunch of times). We wrote down what the transformations gave us and tried to classify them as much as possible before Da-chuan came onto the project as he had been thinking of similar things. One of the things we looked into is how the mixed gravity-duality anomaly in 4d produces projective phases in 3d and the relations to 6d theories compactified on a genus g surface to get \\(\mathrm{U}(1)^g\\) theories. This is also what sparked my interest for anomalies in the first place. Prior to this, my primary interest was in von Neumann algebras. 

I did a decent amount of work towards the anomaly direction and its appearence as a projective phase, but it seemed to not be relevant for the direction our manuscript was heading. Ryan had been using the theories we generated to write down K matrices and associated physical quantities like Hall conductance but ran into the option that, sometimes, one could not integrate out some fields. While I was looking into anomalies, Da-chuan figured out how to deal with the singular K-matrices and also that our setup could explain some unexplained experimental data from 2024. This was very exciting to us and the pace of work increased. To be careful, we had a discussion on when our connections were spin-c/spin (naively a stupid distiction which turns out to be a subtle distiction in 3d for oriented manifolds), mainly by Andreas based on some conversation with Jacques (who was talking with Nati). 

Towards the end of the manuscript, Da-chuan found the missing piece we needed to understand the projective phases for our theory, an order 5 duality instead of the order 3 that is found in the g=1 case. We had a bunch of notes for analyzing such a thing already in an old draft so we filled out the end of section 2 and the appendix discussing the anomaly in less than 2 weeks. This was the the interesting aspect to me and has given me the most ideas of what to do next (based on a lot of what I did in the notes that never made it into the paper). Da-chuan definitely deserves most of the credit for this paper though; without him, I doubt it would be as good as it is. 


## Narain CFTs from nonbinary stabilizer codes
This was my first real project in hep-th. I had very recently gotten interested in AdS/CFT and QECC so I reached out to Tatsuma Nishioka who proposed this program with Narain code CFTs. This is very different from what I wanted to do at the time, but now I really appreciate this project and what it taught me, both physics and skills on how to do research. Kohki-san was a great help to me the entire time as both a colleague and a friend. We learned that we could generate all the RCFTs by picking codes over prime power rings at around 2 AM in the morning while working on Nambu's blackboard. Subsequently, we went for late night gyudon to celebrate. After that the paper sort of fell into place with Okuda-san and Yahagi-san help. Overall, I was very happy with my contribution to this paper. Nishioka-san was of great help and I enjoyed my time in Osaka immensely. At some point, I hope to go back to thinking about the Narain codes.
